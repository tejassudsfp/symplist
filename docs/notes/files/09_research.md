# Simple lists, less maintenance

Researched September 14, 2026.

**Recommendation for someone overwhelmed by maintaining a task system: begin with Today + Later, and use the urgent/important distinction only when choosing what goes into Today.** This is a design recommendation based on your stated needs. The sources below do not establish one universally best list format.

## What the approaches offer

Maintenance estimates and suitability judgments are my assessment, rather than experimental rankings.

| Approach | How it works | Useful when… | Maintenance and limitation | Recommendation for symplist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Today + Later | Keep a small active selection and a holding list. | Seeing every possible task makes choosing difficult. | Low; Later still needs an occasional skim. | Start here; our proposed simplification. |
| Eisenhower matrix | Sort by urgency and importance into four quadrants. | Other people's deadlines or interruptions dominate your attention. | Medium; classifying and reclassifying can become work. | Use as an occasional decision aid. |
| Ivy Lee method | Choose up to six tasks the evening before, rank them, and work through them sequentially. | You want the next day's order decided in advance. | Low; six large tasks can still overfill a day. | Borrow advance selection; start with fewer tasks. |
| GTD next-action lists | Capture commitments, clarify concrete actions, organize, review, and act. | Vague projects or forgotten commitments are the main difficulty. | Medium to high for the full system, which involves multiple lists and reviews. | Borrow concrete next-action wording first. |
| Personal Kanban | Visualize work and limit work in progress; a simple board might be To do / Doing / Done. | You start many things and finish few, or prefer a visual board. | Low to medium; cards need moving and the backlog can grow. | An alternative if a text list feels unhelpful. |

Method descriptions: [Todoist's Eisenhower guide](https://www.todoist.com/productivity-methods/eisenhower-matrix), [James Clear's account of Ivy Lee](https://jamesclear.com/ivy-lee), [official GTD overview](https://gettingthingsdone.com/what-is-gtd/), and [Personal Kanban's two rules](https://personalkanban.com/learn/personal-kanban/). These explain practices; they are not comparative trials proving effectiveness.

## Using the urgent/important matrix without maintaining four lists

Urgent means that delay has a near-term consequence. Important means that it serves something you value or a meaningful responsibility. The conventional matrix recommends doing, scheduling, delegating, or dropping work according to these distinctions. [Source: Eisenhower guide](https://www.todoist.com/productivity-methods/eisenhower-matrix).

The examples and fallback choices below are adaptations for personal use:

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Important** | **Act soon:** submit an application before its deadline. | **Choose time:** draft a portfolio page that supports your longer-term plans. |
| **Less important** | **Delegate, simplify, or renegotiate:** a low-value request that someone wants immediately. | **Drop or park:** an optional workspace redesign. |

When selecting Today, ask: “What has a real consequence if delayed?” Then: “What matters even if nobody is chasing me?” Make room for both where possible.

Delegating is not always available. A necessary chore can deserve a short time slot even when it does little for a long-term goal. Rest, relationships, and enjoyment can be important in their own right.

## What research supports—and what remains a judgment

In a series of experiments, Masicampo and Baumeister found that forming specific plans for unfinished goals reduced the intrusive thoughts and interference those goals produced on other tasks. This supports making a credible plan; it does not establish that merely dumping tasks into a list has the same effect, or that any particular app or list layout is best. [2011 paper abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/).

GTD's useful contribution here is to clarify the next action. A project such as “Launch website” can become “Write three sentences explaining the homepage offer.” Full GTD also involves regular review and a broader organizational process. Borrowing its action wording does not require adopting the entire system. [GTD next-action guidance](https://gettingthingsdone.com/insights/next-action-list/), [GTD weekly review checklist](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Weekly_Review_Checklist.pdf).

Ivy Lee offers a small ordered daily list. James Clear's account specifies six items and acknowledges uncertainty around the historical success story. Treat that story as illustration, not scientific validation. Symplist's suggested one-to-three actions is an adaptation intended to lower the starting burden. [Ivy Lee method](https://jamesclear.com/ivy-lee).

Personal Kanban explicitly combines making work visible with limiting work in progress. Symplist borrows the idea of working on one selected action at a time; a one-item limit is our suggested starting choice. [Personal Kanban](https://personalkanban.com/learn/personal-kanban/).

## Add a list only when something repeatedly falls through

- **Waiting for:** add this only if you often lose track of replies or delegated work. Record the person, expected result, and follow-up date; use a reminder when timing matters.
- **Active projects:** add a short list of outcomes only if you repeatedly lose sight of multi-step commitments. Give each an identifiable next action.
- **Recurring essentials:** use an existing reminder for something you must remember repeatedly. Avoid copying it into the list by hand every day.

For the first week, use the two-section template and an existing calendar. Choose actions small enough to fit the time actually available. If an item keeps rolling forward, ask whether it needs a smaller first step, a missing dependency resolved, a specific time, or removal.

The useful measure is whether this helps you begin and remember what matters with less upkeep. Completing the largest number of checkboxes is not the goal.
